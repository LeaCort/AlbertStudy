Preface
Marketing Fundamentals, the course before this one, told you which customer to pursue and what
your offer is worth. It stopped at the edge of the conversation — the moment the customer sits across
from you and says “your price is too high.” This course is about that conversation. By the end of it
you will be able to do a set of things you cannot reliably do now:
• prepare a negotiation the way professionals do — compute your BATNA, MLATNA and the zone
of possible agreement, and judge whether to create value or claim it;
• separate a party’s stated positions from the interests beneath them, and reopen a stalled negotiation
by trading on those interests;
• apply Cialdini’s six principles and the main buyer biases to build a calibrated, honest argument —
and resist those same principles when they are used on you;
• qualify a B2B opportunity, stress-test it beyond a surface qualification, run a full SPIN discovery,
and apply AIDA to a B2C message;
• handle objections with technique and principle, raise a concern before the buyer does, and disclose
a real product limitation in a way that builds trust;
• diagnose a sales pipeline, find its highest-leverage fixes, and run its conversion rates backwards
from a revenue target to the leads it requires;
• structure a value-based price conversation — anchor, concede, and package deliberately;
• build a plan to keep and grow a client over years, not just to close the first deal;
• hold your own at a crowded, multi-party table and against hard tactics; and
• throughout, tell persuasion from manipulation and decline the manipulative move even when it
would close the deal.
Marketing Fundamentals — segmentation, positioning, value-based pricing — is assumed through-
out and applied, not re-taught.
