Primary sources and further reading
This course draws on a handful of primary sources; each is worth reading in the original.
• Systems thinking. Donella H. Meadows, Thinking in Systems: A Primer (2008) — stocks, flows,
feedback, and suboptimization in plain language. Chapter 1 underpins Chapter 2.
• The Theory of Constraints. Eliyahu M. Goldratt & Jeff Cox, The Goal (1984) — the constraint
idea told as a novel; and Eliyahu M. Goldratt, Theory of Constraints (1990), ch. 1, which states
the Five Focusing Steps formally. Both stand behind Chapter 3. Note Goldratt uses “throughput”
to mean money per unit time, narrower than the units-per-time sense used in Chapter 4 and
Chapter 5.
• Little’s Law. John D. C. Little, “A Proof for the Queuing Formula: 𝐿 = 𝜆𝑊,” Operations Research
9(3), 1961 — the original proof behind Chapter 5.
• Process, capacity, and supply networks. Nigel Slack, Alistair Brandon-Jones & Nicola Burgess,
Operations Management (10th ed., 2022) — the general reference behind Chapter 4, Chapter 6,
Chapter 8, and Chapter 9.
• Service design. Marc Stickdorn et al., This Is Service Design Doing (2018) for blueprints, touch-
points, and recovery; and Frances X. Frei, “The Four Things a Service Business Must Get Right,”
Harvard Business Review, 2008. Both inform Chapter 7.
• The operational MVP. Steve Blank, “Why the Lean Start-Up Changes Everything,” Harvard
Business Review, 2013 — the source for “doing things that don’t scale” behind Chapter 10.
