Preface
Shown a business plan today, you can probably judge whether it sounds appealing. What you cannot
yet do is judge whether the promise it makes can actually be delivered — repeatedly, at the volume
and margin the plan assumes — or name where it will break first when it cannot. This course gives
you that second kind of sight. By the end you will be able to:
• place operations among the business functions, and read how a venture’s company shape —
marketplace, SaaS, DTC, physical services — pre-decides the operation that must deliver it;
• see an operation as a system of stocks and flows, and spot where improving one part degrades
the whole;
• find the single constraint that sets a system’s pace, using the Theory of Constraints, and tell a
physical constraint from a policy or organizational one;
• compute utilization and cycle time, and explain why variability, not busyness alone, is what makes
systems slow;
• apply Little’s Law to estimate lead time and work-in-progress, and show what batching and rework
do to flow;
• design a process and its service experience end to end — decision points, handoffs, touchpoints,
failure points, and recovery paths;
• weigh a make-vs-buy decision on total cost and dependency, not sticker price;
• turn given unit economics into hard bounds on what the operation may cost per unit;
• scope a minimum viable operation, name where and at what volume it breaks first, and justify the
design in writing to a reader who does not speak operations.
Underneath all of it is a single reflex you do not have yet and will not lose: when someone describes
a plan, you trace the flow, locate the constraint, and name the volume at which the design cracks.
That reflex will outlast every formula in these pages.
