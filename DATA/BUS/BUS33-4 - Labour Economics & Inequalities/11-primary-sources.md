Primary sources
Each pointer is the original work behind a result used above; the chapter it serves is named in
brackets.
• G. Becker, Human Capital (1964), and J. Mincer, Schooling, Experience, and Earnings (1974) — the invest-
ment view of education and the wage equation named for Mincer. [Chapter 3]
• M. Spence, “Job Market Signaling”, Quarterly Journal of Economics (1973) — the signalling alternative to
human capital. [Chapter 3]
• M. Bils and P. Klenow, “Does Schooling Cause Growth?”, American Economic Review (2000) — the cross-
country evidence on whether schooling drives growth. [Chapter 3]
• S. Rosen, “Hedonic Prices and Implicit Markets”, Journal of Political Economy (1974), and G. Becker, The
Economics of Discrimination (1957) — compensating differentials and taste-based discrimination. [Chapter 4]
• R. Oaxaca (1973) and A. Blinder (1973) — the wage-gap decomposition that bears their names. [Chapter 4]
• D. Mortensen and C. Pissarides, “Job Creation and Job Destruction in the Theory of Unemployment”, Review of
Economic Studies (1994); C. Pissarides, Equilibrium Unemployment Theory (2000) — the matching model
(Nobel 2010, with P. Diamond). [Chapter 5]
• D. Card and A. Krueger, “Minimum Wages and Employment: A Case Study of the Fast-Food Industry in New
Jersey and Pennsylvania”, American Economic Review (1994), and Myth and Measurement (1995) — the
natural-experiment reframing of the minimum wage. [Chapter 6]
• J. Angrist and A. Krueger, “Does Compulsory School Attendance Affect Schooling and Earnings?”, Quarterly
Journal of Economics (1991) — the quarter-of-birth instrument; J. Angrist and V. Lavy, “Using Maimonides’
Rule to Estimate the Effect of Class Size on Scholastic Achievement”, Quarterly Journal of Economics (1999)
— the regression-discontinuity design; J. Angrist and J.-S. Pischke, Mostly Harmless Econometrics (2009) —
the credibility-revolution toolkit. [Chapter 9]
• T. Piketty, Capital in the Twenty-First Century (2014), and the World Inequality Database (WID.world) —
the 𝑟>𝑔 dynamics and tax-based top shares. [Chapter 7; Chapter 8]
• M. Corak, “Income Inequality, Equality of Opportunity, and Intergenerational Mobility”, Journal of Economic
Perspectives (2013) — the Great Gatsby Curve. [Chapter 8]
• C. Lakner and B. Milanovic, “Global Income Distribution: From the Fall of the Berlin Wall to the Great Recession”,
World Bank Economic Review (2016) — the elephant curve. [Chapter 8]
• D. Card, J. Kluve and A. Weber, “What Works? A Meta Analysis of Recent Active Labor Market Program Evalua-
tions”, Journal of the European Economic Association (2018) — the ALMP evidence. [Chapter 10]
• D. Autor, D. Levy and R. Murnane, “The Skill Content of Recent Technological Change”, Quarterly Journal of
Economics (2003), and D. Autor, “Why Are There Still So Many Jobs?”, Journal of Economic Perspectives
(2015) — the task framework and polarisation. [Chapter 10]
