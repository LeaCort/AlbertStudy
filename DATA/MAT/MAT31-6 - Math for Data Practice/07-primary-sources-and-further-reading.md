Primary sources and further reading
The methods in this book are older than the software that runs them, and each traces back to a
readable original.
• Correlation. K. Pearson (1896), “Mathematical Contributions to the Theory of Evolution. III.
Regression, Heredity, and Panmixia,” Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A — the paper
that fixed the coefficient 𝑟 and the word “regression” in their modern senses.
• Simpson’s paradox. E. H. Simpson (1951), “The Interpretation of Interaction in Contingency
Tables,” Journal of the Royal Statistical Society B — the short note behind Chapter 6.
• The kidney-stone data. C. R. Charig, D. R. Webb, S. R. Payne and J. E. Wickham (1986),
“Comparison of treatment of renal calculi by open surgery, percutaneous nephrolithotomy, and
extracorporeal shockwave lithotripsy,” British Medical Journal — the source of Table 3.
• A modern applied treatment. G. James, D. Witten, T. Hastie and R. Tibshirani, An Introduction
to Statistical Learning (Springer) — Chapter 3 develops simple and multiple linear regression, the
slope’s test, and residual diagnostics without matrix prerequisites.
• A gentle inferential companion. D. Diez, M. Çetinkaya-Rundel and C. Barr, OpenIntro Statistics
(free online) — thorough on standard errors, 𝑡-tests, and the interpretation of 𝑅2.
