Preface
By the end of this course you will be able to do a set of things you cannot do yet. You will read a
mathematical statement — decide exactly what it claims, form its negation, and tell it apart from its
converse. You will prove a statement by each of the standard templates: directly, by contrapositive,
by contradiction, by exhaustion of cases, by induction, and by analysis–synthesis. You will speak
the language of sets and logic, count finite collections with permutations and combinations, and
manipulate sums and products in index notation, including the binomial theorem and the stan-
dard closed forms. You will classify a function as injective, surjective, or bijective, invert it, and work
with images and preimages; and you will compare infinite sets, telling a countable one from an
uncountable one. You will decide whether a sequence converges and compute its limit. Finally, you
will differentiate a function, read its variations and extrema, study its convexity, and integrate it —
using antiderivatives, the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, integration by parts, and substitution.
